## No title. A question instead.

Titles are lagging indicators, so there is none here. What there is: a question that will not leave me alone. What happens when machines start making decisions, and then start building the machines that make the decisions?

We are at the start of an intelligence explosion. Not the science fiction kind, a single flash and a new world. The compounding kind. Models training models. Agents hiring agents. Synthetic minds generating the data that trains the next synthetic minds. Things are going to get crazy, because they already are, quietly, in production systems, one deployment at a time.

The only sane response is to stay curious, stay in the arena, and build the instruments: the tests, the measurements, the tripwires, the proofs. That is what this page is. A lab notebook from the foothills of the explosion.

My corner of it is the adversarial one: fraud, identity, and the question of who, or what, is actually on the other side of a transaction. Everything here runs on synthetic or public data. No production data, no real transactions, no real people, anywhere on this profile.

## Dispatches from the intelligence explosion

Field notes on what is actually arriving, and what I am building while it does:

- **Agents are getting identities.** They are about to open accounts, move money, and sign on your behalf. So who verifies the agent? See [know-your-agent](https://github.com/RishabhPande/know-your-agent).
- **Agents are getting evaluated, badly.** Leaderboards run once and crown whoever got lucky. See [agentstats](https://github.com/RishabhPande/agentstats) and [agentrisk](https://github.com/RishabhPande/agentrisk).
- **Agents are getting memories, and memories can be poisoned.** Corrupt the memory, and you never have to corrupt the mind. See [memory-poison-lab](https://github.com/RishabhPande/memory-poison-lab).
- **Agents fail quietly and retry loudly.** Persistence without memory is just expensive stubbornness. See [agent-retry-rodeo](https://github.com/RishabhPande/agent-retry-rodeo).
- **Fraud is becoming a graph problem fought by graphs.** Rings hide in transaction networks; graph networks go in after them. See [GNN-fraud-ring-detection](https://github.com/RishabhPande/GNN-fraud-ring-detection).
- **Detectors lie politely. Benchmarks should not.** See [fraud-eval](https://github.com/RishabhPande/fraud-eval).
- **The basics compound.** One algorithm a day, mastered slowly, beats a hundred shallow tours. See [tiny-ml-lab](https://github.com/RishabhPande/tiny-ml-lab).

## This week in the lab: November 20, 2026

[fraud-eval](https://github.com/RishabhPande/fraud-eval/pull/3) now reports caught detector exceptions beside its metrics. A failed call can still count as a true negative under the existing fallback policy; the error count makes that visible. Exception text stays out of the results.

[Feature Contracts](https://github.com/RishabhPande/feature-contracts/pull/6) keeps positive-infinite evidence in its top-feature explanations instead of dropping the feature that broke the contract. Detection flags and calibration are unchanged.

[agentstats](https://github.com/RishabhPande/agentstats/pull/10) counts leaderboard pair flips one sampled leaderboard at a time, cutting temporary pair-array allocation. Same statistics and tie handling; pair comparisons still grow quadratically.

I also fixed [Tiny ML Lab](https://github.com/RishabhPande/tiny-ml-lab/pull/6)'s spectral-clustering failure demo. It now checks the erased graph, not an arbitrary bad accuracy from a degenerate eigenspace. The clustering algorithm and learner workbook are unchanged. All 116 tests pass.

## Fork this if

- **... you want to beat a seed sweep.** In [GNN-fraud-ring-detection](https://github.com/RishabhPande/GNN-fraud-ring-detection)'s synthetic demo setup, mean aggregation drops to AUC 0.728 / 0.539 on seeds 8-9 while max-pool, LSTM and GIN aggregators hold 1.000. Synthetic data, three seeds: a demo result, not a benchmark. Add your own aggregator and run the sweep. Pure Python, zero dependencies.
- **... you want a workbook, not a gallery.** [tiny-ml-lab](https://github.com/RishabhPande/tiny-ml-lab) is built to be forked: fill in each workbook.py, check days off in your own PROGRESS.md, add a row to the SHOWCASE.md hall of fame, and vote the next algorithm up the queue in REQUESTS.md.
- **... you want an unclaimed task.** [Time-series-anomaly-detection](https://github.com/RishabhPande/Time-series-anomaly-detection) has four on its roadmap: multivariate series with cross-channel attention, Informer-style sparse attention for long windows, streaming inference without re-encoding the window, and learned threshold calibration. Pick one and open a PR.
